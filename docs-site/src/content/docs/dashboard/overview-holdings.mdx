## Overview

The Overview tab is the first thing you see: portfolio value, daily P&L, sector
breakdown, and a market regime briefing. It's built for a five-second read — is the
portfolio up, down, and by roughly how much, before you've even decided to look closer.

## Holdings

The Holdings tab is where the verdict engine lives. Each position gets:

- A **Hold / Add / Trim / Exit**-style verdict
- A **confidence** level and **time horizon**
- **Scenario context** — what would need to change for the verdict to flip

Rows expand on the first click (no more double-clicking out of habit) and auto-refresh
keeps prices current without a manual reload.

At phone widths the filter and action controls stack into a content-sized header, so the
first holding row remains reachable without scrolling through a desktop-sized spacer.

### Source candidate: incomplete prices

Proposed for the next release: every saved row remains available through a price outage,
including its details and management controls. Unknown prices and price comparisons say
**Unavailable**. A partial book shows **Known USD value**; whole-portfolio return and
allocation comparisons wait for complete coverage. Foreign price and value cells retain
native units such as `GBp` or `CAD` and stay out of USD comparisons. A recovered quote
updates the same holding rather than creating another row.

In the same source candidate, holding edits save one request at a time. If a connection
fails, FolioOrb reads back the saved holding and any affected realized history before a
dependent edit or removal. Newer input stays on screen as an unsaved draft. If readback is
unreachable, the save remains **Status unknown**; editing again checks the saved facts before
sending another change. Cancelling a sale confirmation sends no sale request.

<SenpaiTip>
	Toggle Research mode when adding a ticker to follow the idea without treating it as money
	you own — it stays out of P&L, allocation, largest-holding stats, briefings, and action-plan
	buckets until you add shares.
</SenpaiTip>

Research-only rows remain visible in Holdings and News, but the Overview's invested-position
count and performance tiles ignore them. If a portfolio contains only research ideas, FolioOrb
says there are no invested positions instead of manufacturing a portfolio read.

## Holding intelligence

Expanding a ticker surfaces deeper context:

- Peer-relative positioning within its sector
- Event flags (earnings, dividends, and other calendar items)
- Contribution breakdown — how much of your P&L this position is actually responsible for
- **Insider activity** — recent open-market buys and sells by the company's own officers
  and directors, from SEC Form 4
- **Financials over time** — revenue, net margin, and diluted EPS by fiscal year, from
  what the company actually filed
- **Your thesis** — the reason you own it, in your own words
- Equity or ETF-specific detail depending on the holding type

The earnings radar carries the consensus **EPS estimate** for the upcoming report and how
often the company has beaten that estimate over the last four quarters — so "reports in 3
days" comes with the bar it's expected to clear. Coverage is thin for some tickers; when
there's no estimate, the date still shows and the app just doesn't guess.

The **insider activity** panel counts only open-market buys and sells as conviction — the
signal that someone chose to put their own money in or take it out. Option exercises, grants,
gifts, and tax withholding are shown for completeness but never counted, because they aren't
decisions to buy or sell at the market. Funds and ETFs have no insiders, so they say so.

The **financials** panel reads SEC XBRL data straight from filings — revenue bars scaled to
the window's own peak, with net margin and EPS beside each year. A metric a company didn't
file shows an em dash, never a fabricated zero, and the revenue history stitches across the
GAAP tag change most large filers made around 2018.

The **Your thesis** box is the one panel the app has no opinion about. Write why you bought
the thing — the reason, the condition that would change your mind, whatever you'd want to
hear from yourself later. Conviction is easy to articulate on a green day and very hard to
reconstruct in a drawdown, which is exactly when you'll want to read it back.

It's yours alone: Claude never reads or writes it, it stays in your local database like
everything else, and it round-trips through CSV import and export so it survives a move
between machines. Editing happens inline, and a background refresh can't clobber what you're
typing — if a save fails, your words stay on screen instead of disappearing.

Open **Review → Inbox** to put that thesis on an optional cadence. Mark it reviewed and
choose 30, 60, 90, 180, or 365 days; FolioOrb brings it back when due. Cadence affects only
the local review workflow—it never changes Portfolio math, a verdict, or what Claude sees.

None of this requires Claude. It's all part of Local Intelligence, running deterministically
against live market data. Claude, when connected, adds narration on top — not a replacement
for the underlying numbers.

## DCA plans and review decisions

Open the portfolio manager (press **M**) and choose **DCA** to mirror a recurring buy without
connecting a brokerage. Each due interval waits in a local review bucket until you apply or
skip it. Applied buys can be undone exactly only while their linked holding still contains
the recorded shares and basis; later sales or edits can block reversal, leaving both the
holding and DCA ledger unchanged.

Bulk apply, undo, skip, amount changes, and plan deletion use FolioOrb dialogs rather than
browser-native prompts. Each dialog states what changes and what stays, keeps keyboard focus
inside until you decide, cancels with Escape, and returns focus to the action you started
from. A changed amount affects future intervals only. A plan cannot be deleted while it has
applied buys: undo those buys first, then deletion removes its pending and skipped entries.
If deletion is refused, FolioOrb shows the ledger's reason in-app instead of silently doing
nothing.

## Import & export (CSV)

Open the portfolio manager (press **M**) and you'll find **Export CSV** and **Import
CSV** next to the holdings search.

**Export** downloads your active holdings — positions and watchlist alike — as a clean
CSV with six columns: `ticker, shares, avg_cost, is_watchlist, hold_class, notes`. It
opens correctly in Excel, and it *is* the import template, so whatever you export imports
straight back in.

**Current source builds — review, then confirm.** Open **Import CSV → Preview CSV…** to
inspect up to 200 strict template rows for the selected portfolio, 25 rows per page. The table
shows proposed persisted values and duplicate/validation/provider reasons. Cost 0 means an
unknown cost under the existing storage convention; unset thesis cadence is shown as —.
Unrecognized research flags still become false. Entered shares and costs, and shares
multiplied by average cost, must be finite. Confirmation also checks stored financial totals
before committing. Preview does not calculate or approve portfolio totals.

**Confirm N additions** saves exactly the reviewed valid additions in one transaction;
invalid/skipped rows stay excluded. A preview expires after 15 minutes, and the process keeps
at most 16 outstanding preparations/previews and 16 successful replay receipts. Changed
selected holdings or portfolio identity require a fresh preview. Database busy permits an
explicit manual retry; the app never retries the write automatically. **Close preview**
before confirmation discards the view. Closing while confirmation is running does not undo
a save or prove the request stopped.

**If the outcome is uncertain:** choose **Refresh and compare reviewed rows**. After a
reload, reselect the original reviewed CSV. Comparison checks ticker, shares, average cost,
research state, classification, notes and thesis cadence against the current selected
portfolio. It shows reviewed→current values as matching, mismatching or unknown; matching
records do not prove this request created them. Inspect every field, acknowledge the
comparison, then explicitly choose **Start a fresh preview**. Missing records are a known
mismatch; unknown state keeps a fresh import blocked. Comparison cannot undo records and
sends no import. A verified save followed by a failed display refresh says **Saved, but
refresh failed**; compare instead of confirming again.

The same tab retains bounded recovery metadata derived from the reviewed file (digests and
row numbers), not the file, token, tickers, notes or holdings values. Server restart
invalidates tokens. A restored/replaced database blocks old-process CSV operations until
restart; then compare the original CSV. Tab closure can lose session metadata. There is no
durable idempotency ledger or exactly-once guarantee across crashes.

Preview parses locally and never calls Claude. Ticker validation may send symbols to Yahoo
Finance; the full CSV and notes are not sent there. Confirmation/comparison use no provider
or AI. **Import immediately…** remains separate and saves valid rows when a file is chosen.
These source preview/confirmation features are not in the existing v5.16.1 installers;
their existing file-choice action imports immediately. Native acceptance/publication remain
separate gates.

**Import** reads holdings back in, and how forgiving it is depends on whether Claude is
connected — the panel tells you which mode you're in:

- **Local mode (no API key needed).** Uses the exact template above: plain numbers, the
  six known columns, up to 200 rows. Parsing is local; ticker validation can contact Yahoo Finance. If your file
  has columns it doesn't recognize, it says so plainly and points you at the template —
  it never fails silently.
- **Claude assist (when a key is configured).** Drop in almost any brokerage export —
  differing headers like `Symbol` or `Qty`, currency symbols, extra columns — and Claude
  maps the columns onto the FolioOrb format for you. Every row still passes the *same*
  strict checks before it's added, so Claude only ever *widens* what's accepted; it never
  skips validation. Clean template files skip Claude entirely (and cost nothing), and if
  Claude is ever slow or unavailable the import falls back to the strict local parse and
  tells you it did.

Either way, you get a per-row summary: what was added, what was skipped (existing holdings
are skipped, never overwritten), and what errored and why. In Claude mode, Senpai adds a
one-line recap on top.

<SenpaiTip>
	Editing a CSV in Excel? Watch out for tickers like `MAR26` — Excel loves to "helpfully"
	reformat those into dates. If a row bounces on import with a date-looking ticker, format
	that column as **Text** and re-export.
</SenpaiTip>
